# Tone guidance

Your letter's tone decides how it's received. A calm, clear letter gets taken
seriously. An angry or confusing one gets dismissed. This chapter shows you
how to strike the right balance.

## The golden rule

**Be polite, be specific, be brief.** You can be firm without being rude.

## Four tone qualities to aim for

| Quality | What it means | Example |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Polite | Respectful to the reader, even when you're upset | "I am writing to request..." not "You people messed up..." |
| Specific | Names, dates, account references, not vague feelings | "On March 3, I was charged $42.00 twice" not "You keep overcharging me" |
| Brief | One page or less when possible | Short paragraphs, one idea per paragraph |
| Clear about what you want | Say exactly what outcome you need | "Please refund $42.00 to my account by April 15" |

## Tone by letter type

### Complaint letters: firm but respectful

You are upset, and that's fine. Don't let anger show in the letter.
- DO describe what happened, when, and what you want fixed.
- DON'T insult the company, call names, or threaten.
- Example opener: "I am writing about a billing error on my account from March 3."

### Appeal letters: calm and factual

You are asking someone to reverse a decision. Emotion works against you here.
- DO stick to facts, dates, and policy language if you have it.
- DON'T beg or accuse.
- Example opener: "I am writing to appeal the denial of claim #[NUMBER], dated [DATE]."

### Thank-you notes: warm and sincere

Keep it simple and personal.
- DO name what you're thanking them for and why it mattered.
- DON'T overdo it — three to five sentences is often plenty.
- Example opener: "Thank you for seeing me on Tuesday and explaining my test results."

### Request letters: direct and easy to say yes to

Make it effortless for the reader to help you.
- DO say what you need, why, and by when (if relevant).
- DON'T demand or assume.
- Example opener: "I am writing to request a copy of my medical records from [DATES]."

## Words to use and avoid

| Use instead | Avoid |
|-------------|-------|
| I am writing to request | I demand |
| I would appreciate | You must |
| Unfortunately | This is ridiculous |
| Could you please | You people |
| I understand | As everyone knows |
| Please let me know | I expect immediate |

## Read it out loud

Before sending, read your draft out loud. If a sentence makes you wince or
sound angry, rewrite it. If you wouldn't say it to a person's face, don't put
it in a letter.
