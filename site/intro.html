Introduction

Why write letters with AI?

AI is good at the part most people dread: the blank page. It can help you organize your thoughts, choose polite words, and keep your letter short and clear. You still provide the facts — the dates, names, and what happened — and you always review the final draft before sending.

Who this is for

Anyone who has ever stared at a blank screen trying to write a letter. You don't need to be technical. You don't need to have used AI before. If you can copy and paste, you can use this guide.

This guide was written by Dylan Asdale, a registered nurse turned AI author and founder of Asdale Dynamics, LLC. His book The Massive AI Manual and hub site dylanasdale.github.io explain AI "like a friend would": plain English, step by step, never condescending.

A safe, step-by-step process

Step 1: Know what you want

Before you open an AI chat, write down (on paper or in a notes app):

  • Who you are writing to
  • Why you are writing
  • What outcome you want (a refund, an apology, a meeting, records, etc.)
  • The key dates and facts

Step 2: Pick your template

Open the chapter for your letter type: complaint, appeal, thank-you, or request. Each chapter has a fill-in-the-blank template.

Step 3: Fill in your facts

Replace every [BRACKETED PLACEHOLDER] with your real details. Use short, simple sentences. Stick to what actually happened.

Step 4: Use the AI prompt

Copy the prompt from the AI prompt templates chapter (or the end of your letter chapter). Paste it into your AI chat tool and fill in the bracketed parts with your facts. Ask the AI to write a draft.

Step 5: Read and edit

Read the AI's draft out loud. Fix anything wrong. Make sure it sounds like you. Remove anything you don't want to say.

Step 6: Check the "never include" list

Before sending, compare your draft against What never to include. Remove anything on that list.

Step 7: Send or print

Send by email, mail, or fax — whatever the recipient asks for. Or print from templates/printable/ for a paper copy.

What AI is good at here

  • Turning rough notes into a polite, organized letter
  • Suggesting subject lines and openings
  • Keeping the tone calm and professional
  • Shortening long, rambling drafts

What AI is not good at

  • Knowing the true facts of your situation (only you do)
  • Understanding your legal rights or medical needs
  • Guaranteeing a specific outcome
  • Keeping secrets safe (assume anything you paste into an AI tool could be seen by others)
